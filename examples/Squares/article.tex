\section{The square}

Define $q:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ by $q(x)\coloneqq x^2$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:nonneg}
For every $x\in\mathbb{R}$, $q(x)\ge0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:amgm}
For every $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$, $2xy\le q(x)+q(y)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For every $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$, $q(x)+q(y)-2xy=q(x-y)$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:nonneg}, $q(x-y)\ge0$ for every $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$.
\end{proof}

The theorem is the quadratic form of the inequality between the arithmetic and the geometric mean.
It holds for every pair of real numbers, without any sign condition.
